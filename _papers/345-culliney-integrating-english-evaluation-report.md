abstract: >-
  The Integrating English programme aimed to improve the writing
  ability of Year 5 and 6 pupils through a structured CPD
  programme (based on the Australian LiLAC course) that provided
  teachers with linguistic and pedagogical strategies, with the
  greatest expected impact on EAL pupils. This was a two-arm,
  school-level clustered randomised controlled trial involving 91
  schools, run between October 2016 and July 2018, with schools
  randomly allocated to receive Integrating English or to
  continue as a 'business as usual' control. Children in
  Integrating English schools made the equivalent of one month's
  less progress in KS2 writing on average compared to control
  schools, with similarly null or small negative effects on KS2
  Reading and KS2 Grammar, Punctuation and Spelling; none of
  these findings were statistically significant. The process
  evaluation found training was well received but only a
  minority of schools fully implemented the programme as
  intended.

      Relevant Quotes:

      1) "This was a randomised controlled trial involving 91
      schools. Schools were randomly allocated to either receive
      the Integrating English programme, or to be in a 'business
      as usual' control." (p. 5)

      2) "The impact evaluation design was a two-arm, school-
      level clustered randomised controlled trial (CRCT). School
      level randomisation was used as it is more practical for
      recruitment and implementation. It also eliminates the
      risk of overspill, which is crucial for ensuring
      robustness." (p. 14)

      3) "The MinimPy programme was used to allocate schools
      into the intervention and control groups using
      minimisation with a random start. Five school-level
      variables were used to ensure a good baseline balance
      between the two groups." (p. 16)

      Detailed Analysis:

      The ERCT 'C' criterion requires randomisation at the class
      level or stronger, with the whole school level explicitly
      accepted as satisfying (indeed exceeding) this requirement.
      The paper is explicit and consistent throughout that the
      unit of randomisation was the whole school, not individual
      classes or students within a school. Ninety-one schools
      were recruited and then randomly allocated using a
      minimisation algorithm to intervention or control status,
      with entire year groups (all Y5, then all Y6, classes)
      within intervention schools receiving the programme. This
      is a considerably stronger design than class-level
      randomisation and removes any risk of within-school
      contamination between conditions.

      Since school-level randomisation automatically satisfies
      the weaker class-level criterion under the ERCT standard,
      criterion C is met.

      Applying the decision procedure for criterion B: the extra
      resource here is teacher continuing professional
      development (five days of LiLAC training per year group,
      approximately 50 hours total plus supported scheme-of-work
      preparation), which is delivered to teachers outside pupil
      class time; teaching to pupils itself occurs within
      "normal classroom time" for both intervention and control

      budget, or class time given to intervention pupils relative
      to control pupils. The additional resource that is not
      matched (teacher training) is integral to, and is precisely,
      the treatment variable under investigation -- the trial
      is testing whether a professional development programme
      changes teaching practice and pupil outcomes, not whether
      extra pupil-facing time or materials improve outcomes.
      Control schools were kept on a "business as usual" footing
      and additionally received a modest £200 payment,
      consistent with the standard EEF practice of incentivising
      participation without providing a competing intervention.

      Because the differential resource (teacher training) is the
      explicit subject of the treatment being tested and pupils
      in both arms received equivalent classroom time and
      materials, criterion B is met.
