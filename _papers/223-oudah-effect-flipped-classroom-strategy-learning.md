abstract: >-
  The study identifies the effect of the flipped classroom
  strategy and the command method in learning certain basic
  skills in football for second-grade students in the middle
  stage, and the preference of the effect between the flipped
  classroom strategy and the command method in learning some
  skills in football. The experimental method was used with
  the pre- and post-tests to fit the research problem as the
  best way to achieve its goals. The study population was (90)
  intermediate students of the second-grade intermediate
  leadership. The study sampled (60) students who were selected
  through the lottery system for the two divisions (A, C), and
  they represent 66% of the original population, and the most
  important results of the research were - The flipped
  classroom strategy is superior to the command method in the
  performance of rolling and handling skills of the second
  grade students in the middle stage.- Enhancing the positive
  role of the student, achieving harmony with the study
  material, and creating a spirit of competition and
  enthusiasm, which led to an increase in classroom
  interaction.

      Detailed Analysis:

      Criterion B asks whether any additional time, budget or
      materials given to the intervention group are matched in
      the control group, unless the additional resource is
      itself the treatment variable being tested. Both groups
      here study the same two football skills within the same
      physical-education class time: the experimental group via
      the flipped classroom method (content delivered by video
      at home, class time used for application and discussion),
      and the control group via the command method (in-class
      teacher explanation). The distinguishing feature of the
      flipped condition is that content delivery is shifted to
      out-of-class video viewing, which does add a home-study
      component not present for the control group.

      However, this out-of-class video component is not a
      separable add-on: it is the defining, integral feature of
      the flipped classroom strategy, which is exactly the
      treatment variable under investigation (the study's stated
      aim is to compare the flipped classroom strategy against
      the command method). The in-class instructional time is
      comparable across the two conditions, and the flipped
      design deliberately relocates the explanatory content that
      the command method delivers in class. Applying the
      criterion B decision tree, the extra resource (home video
      study) is integral to the intervention being tested, so
      the control group's business-as-usual command instruction
      does not constitute an unbalancing confound.

      Criterion B is met because the only additional resource in
      the intervention group (out-of-class video content
      delivery) is the integral, defining feature of the flipped
      classroom strategy being tested, while in-class time is
      comparable to the command-method control.
