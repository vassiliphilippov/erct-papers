  Teaching Grammar and Writing: A Randomised Controlled

  Englicious

abstract: >-
  Very few research studies of the teaching of grammar and
  writing had been carried out with children younger than
  eight-years-old prior to the research reported in this
  paper. The research evaluated a new approach to teaching
  grammar and writing called Englicious. A Randomized
  Controlled Trial (RCT) and Implementation and Process
  Evaluation (IPE) research design, featuring 1,246 pupils
  aged six to seven-years-old in 70 primary school classes,
  was used to evaluate the effectiveness of Englicious for
  improving children's writing. The approach was
  implemented in the context of the programs of study for
  grammar teaching in England's national curriculum. The
  research found that there was no effect of the grammar
  teaching intervention on pupils' narrative writing. The
  effect size for pupils' sentence generation was
  sufficient to merit reflections about potential impacts
  of aspects of the intervention although this outcome also
  did not reach statistical significance. It is
  hypothesised that the manipulation of words, phrases and
  sentences, combined with practice at writing, may have
  contributed to any positive effect, although this would
  need to be confirmed in future research. It is concluded
  that until more research is done to investigate the
  effectiveness of different approaches to teaching grammar
  and writing with young children, existing evidence-based
  approaches are more likely to be effective to help young
  pupils' narrative writing. England's national curriculum
  specifications for teaching grammar and writing could
  usefully be reviewed to more closely reflect the evidence
  base from this field of writing research.

      one class per school participated, randomising the
      school is equivalent to randomising the class, and it
      is at least as strong as class-level assignment,
      preventing within-class contamination. The ERCT
      standard states that when a study is a school-level
      RCT, the weaker class-level criterion is considered
      met.

      budget, or materials not matched in the control, and
      if so whether those additional inputs are integral to

      received a one-day teacher professional development
      session and lessons that combined explicit grammar
      teaching with an embedded writing-practice element,
      whereas control teachers continued business-as-usual
      grammar teaching required by the same statutory
      national curriculum. Both groups were obliged to teach
      the same grammar content and delivered grammar within
      normal curriculum time; the distinguishing feature of
      the intervention is the specific Englicious pedagogy
      (grammar teaching directly linked to writing
      practice). The study's stated hypothesis makes this
      combined approach the explicit treatment variable
      being tested against a business-as-usual baseline, so
      the additional writing practice and the associated
      teacher training are integral to the intervention
      rather than separable confounds. Under the ERCT
      decision rule, when the added inputs are integral to
      the treatment being tested against business-as-usual,
      the criterion is satisfied.

      Criterion B is met because the additional writing
      practice and teacher training are integral components
      of the Englicious approach that constitutes the
      explicit treatment variable, compared against a
      business-as-usual control.
