abstract: >-
  Since English has been deemed a foreign language in
  Indonesian schools, how EFL students learn and acquire
  English vocabulary has been a hot topic. The purpose of
  this study is to demonstrate the impact of WLI on
  Indonesian EFL students' vocabulary gains and to discover
  how students feel about the usage of a web-based method
  in language instruction. Students were randomly assigned
  to one of two groups: the Experimental group (EG = 30),
  which used free vocabulary learning webpages on the
  IELTS English language learning site each day for 12
  weeks, and the control group (CG = 30), which got regular
  classroom teaching every session. This was done to make
  sure that the groups were homogeneous. By introducing
  web-based training into the curriculum, it seemed that
  students perceptions of language acquisition had
  improved. The findings showed a considerable disparity in
  vocabularies among EG and CG. The vocabulary of EFL
  students improved as a result of WBL education. This
  study results might have some benefits on how languages
  are taught and learned.

      Detailed Analysis:

      The EG received additional resources not given to the
      CG: a personal laptop, 24-hour computer/internet
      access, and a specific web-based (IELTS) vocabulary
      tool for independent, self-paced study. Applying the
      criterion B decision procedure: extra resources are
      present (laptop, internet access); however, the paper
      explicitly frames "the use of the Internet as a
      collaborative tool" as the independent variable itself
      -- i.e., the very thing being tested is whether
      web-based instruction (WLI), including its associated
      hardware and internet access, outperforms conventional
      classroom teaching. These resources are therefore
      INTEGRAL to the treatment variable being tested, not a
      supplementary, separable add-on. The CG, by contrast,
      received "the same teaching process" it would
      ordinarily receive (regular classroom instruction),
      representing the business-as-usual baseline against
      which the web-based tool is compared. Because the
      additional resources are integral to and inseparable
      from the treatment variable under investigation, the
      study is not required to give the CG equivalent
      hardware/internet access.

      Criterion B is met because the additional resources
      (laptop and internet-based vocabulary tool) given to
      the EG are the explicit treatment variable being
      tested against a business-as-usual classroom-teaching
      control condition.
