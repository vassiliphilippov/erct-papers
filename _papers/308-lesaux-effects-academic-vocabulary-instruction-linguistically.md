title: >
  Effects of Academic Vocabulary Instruction for
  Linguistically Diverse Adolescents: Evidence From a
  Randomized Field Trial
authors: >
  Nonie K. Lesaux, Michael J. Kieffer, Joan G. Kelley,

paper_link: "https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0002831214532165"
abstract: >-
  We conducted a randomized field trial to test an
  academic vocabulary intervention designed to bolster
  the language and literacy skills of linguistically
  diverse sixth-grade students (N = 2,082; n = 1,469
  from a home where English is not the primary
  language), many demonstrating low achievement,
  enrolled in 14 urban middle schools. The 20-week
  classroom-based intervention improved students'
  vocabulary knowledge, morphological awareness skills,
  and comprehension of expository texts that included
  academic words taught, as well as their performance on
  a standardized measure of written language skills. The
  effects were generally larger for students whose
  primary home language is not English and for those
  students who began the intervention with
  underdeveloped vocabulary knowledge.

    analysis: >-
      Relevant Quotes:

      1) "This cluster-randomized field trial was
      conducted in 14 middle schools in a large, urban
      school district in California that serves an
      economically and linguistically diverse student
      population. Fifty teachers and 2,082 students
      participated. The cluster-randomized trial meant
      that each of these 50 teachers was assigned to
      either the treatment or control condition (the
      method for random assignment is described below in
      the Study Design section)." (p. 1166)

      2) "The cluster-randomized trial required that each
      of the 50 teachers be assigned to the treatment or
      control condition. Within each school, teachers
      were blocked on achievement based on the classroom
      mean score on the state standards test in ELA
      administered at the end of the prior academic year,
      and then randomized within blocks..." (p. 1167)

      in 39 sections were assigned to the control
      condition and 25 teachers along with their students
      in 37 sections were assigned to the treatment
      condition." (p. 1166)

      Detailed Analysis:

      The unit of randomization was the teacher, not
      individual students within a shared classroom.
      Because every section (class) taught by a given
      teacher moved together as a block to either
      treatment or control, there is no within-classroom
      mixing of conditions, and thus no risk of the
      contamination this criterion is designed to
      prevent. This is at least as strong as class-level
      randomization, satisfying the requirement even
      though it stops short of the stronger school-level
      design (each school contained both treatment and
      control teachers).

      Criterion C is met because randomization was
      conducted at the teacher/class-cluster level, with
      whole teachers (and their sections) assigned to a
      single condition.

      Applying the decision procedure: the intervention
      did add extra teacher-facing resources (monthly
      coaching meetings with program specialists, a

      control-condition teachers did not receive. However,
      the student-facing instructional time and the
      general skills targeted (reading, writing,
      listening, speaking) were explicitly matched across
      conditions, since ALIAS was designed as a
      replacement for, not an addition to, regular ELA
      class time. The extra professional-development
      support for treatment teachers is not a separable,
      confounding add-on but an integral component of
      training teachers to deliver the specific ALIAS
      curriculum under evaluation, analogous to teacher
      training resources considered integral in other
      accepted cases (see ERCT spec examples on teacher
      professional development as part of the treatment
      package). There is no evidence that the control
      group's core educational time or budget was reduced
      relative to the treatment group; if anything,
      control teachers were rated somewhat higher on
      general instructional quality, indicating no
      systematic disadvantage to the control condition.

      Criterion B is met because student instructional
      time was balanced across conditions, and the
      additional teacher support given to the treatment
      group is integral to delivering the tested
      curriculum rather than an unmatched supplementary
      resource.
