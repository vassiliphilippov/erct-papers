title: >
  The Effect of Teaching Discourse Markers through Flipped
  Model on Iraqi EFL Learners' Writing Performance
authors: >

paper_link: "https://doi.org/10.32038/ltrq.2024.45.07"
abstract: >-
  The main purpose of this study was to investigate the effect
  of discourse markers instruction through the flipped model on
  Iraqi EFL learners' writing performance. To achieve this end,
  90 advanced EFL learners were selected and assigned into three
  groups; two as the experimental and one as the control group.
  The homogeneity of the participants was checked and the
  normality of the data was calculated. Data analysis revealed
  that teaching discourse markers significantly affected
  learners' knowledge of discourse markers. However, there was
  no significant effect of teaching discourse markers on
  advanced Iraqi EFL learners' overall writing ability. The
  results of the interview showed that using flipped model
  increased learners' confidence and developed positive
  attitudes towards flipped classes. Finally, some of the
  challenges and the problems of the study were stated by the
  interviewees. The findings of the present study can be
  fruitful for practitioners the field of EFL teaching in
  general, and for those who plan to hold flipped classes in
  particular.

      4) "In all the three groups, during the classroom time, the
      students were given an expository topic to write about and
      then, the writing of one of the students was examined and
      commented by the teacher and all other students every
      session." (Data Collection Procedure)

      Detailed Analysis:

      All three groups received identical in-class time each
      session (90 minutes/week) devoted to writing the same
      expository prompts with teacher feedback; the only
      structural difference across groups is whether, and how,
      discourse-marker instruction was delivered. The first
      experimental group additionally received brief pre-session
      audio/video/PowerPoint clips two to three days beforehand.
      This extra take-home resource is not a separable,
      confounding add-on: it is precisely how the "flipped model"
      treatment is operationally defined in this study, and the
      three-way comparison (flipped delivery vs. traditional
      in-class delivery vs. no discourse-marker instruction) is
      the explicit research question (RQ1/RQ2). Per the standard's
      decision logic, when the additional resource is itself the
      treatment variable being tested, the other conditions may
      remain at their respective baselines without violating
      balance. The traditional and control groups, which are not
      the ones defined by the extra resource, are already matched
      to each other in classroom time and task structure.

      Criterion B is met because the only additional resource
      (short take-home media clips for the flipped group) is
      integral to the flipped-model treatment under
      investigation, while in-class time and task structure are
      otherwise equal across all three groups.
