title: >
  The Effect of the Flipped Classroom Model and the
  Argumentation-Based Learning Model on Students' Academic
  Achievement and Attitudes toward the Social Studies Course

  Yavuz Selim Seçgin, Suat Polat

abstract: >-
  The aim of this study is to examine the effects of the Flipped
  Classroom Model and the Argumentation-Based Learning Model on
  sixth-grade students' academic achievement and attitudes toward
  the Social Studies course. The study was conducted in the spring
  semester of the 2023-2024 academic year with 111 students: 31 in
  the Argumentation-Based Learning Group (ABLG), 28 in the Flipped
  Classroom Group (FCG), and two control groups receiving Ministry
  of National Education curriculum-based instruction, Control
  Group 1 (CG1) and Control Group 2 (CG2), each consisting of 26
  students. The implementation lasted eight weeks, including a
  one-week orientation period and a seven-week intervention. A
  quantitative research approach was employed using the Solomon
  Four-Group experimental design. Normality was examined through
  skewness and kurtosis values. Between-group comparisons were
  analyzed using independent-samples t-tests, and the findings
  were interpreted together with effect-size estimates.

      Detailed Analysis:

      Criterion C requires that the study be a randomised
      controlled trial in which the randomisation process is
      clearly described and properly implemented, with entire
      classes (or schools) as the unit of allocation.

      The sole basis for a randomisation claim is the letter "R"
      in the "Assignment" column of Table 1, glossed in the table
      note as "R = random assignment". Critically, the paper
      itself introduces that table as "the symbolic representation
      of the solomon four-group design" - that is, Table 1 is the
      canonical textbook schematic of the Solomon design, not a
      report of how allocation was actually carried out in this
      trial. The "R" therefore documents a design template rather
      than an implemented procedure. No section of the manuscript
      describes any randomisation mechanism: there is no random
      number generator, randomisation list, sequence generation,
      blocking, stratification, allocation concealment, or
      statement of who performed the allocation.

      The narrative text actively contradicts a random-allocation
      reading. The Study Group section states that "Convenience
      sampling was used to determine the school and classes", and
      the sentence describing allocation says four sixth-grade
      classes "were assigned to the study groups" - conspicuously
      without the word "randomly". Four intact classes in a single
      conveniently selected school were mapped onto the four
      Solomon cells, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that
      procedural detail was omitted ("This concise reporting was
      preferred to avoid excessive procedural detail"). Even if
      allocation of the four intact classes to the four cells had
      been random, four clusters is far too few for randomisation
      to balance confounders, and no baseline data at all exist
      for the two non-pre-tested arms (FCG, CG2) to check this.

      Following the standard's procedure, where the unit and
      process of randomisation are unclear, additional quotes must
      clarify the randomisation steps; here the additional quotes
      point to convenience sampling instead. The intervention is
      whole-class instruction rather than personal tutoring, so
      the tutoring exception does not apply.

      Criterion C is not met because the randomisation claim rests
      only on an "R" symbol in a generic Solomon design schematic,
      no allocation procedure is described anywhere, and the
      Method text reports convenience sampling of the school and
      the four intact classes.
