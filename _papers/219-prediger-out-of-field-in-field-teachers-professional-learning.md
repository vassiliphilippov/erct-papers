title: >
  Out-of-field and in-field teachers' professional learning
  gains on fostering understanding of multiplication:
  A randomized controlled trial
authors: >
  Susanne Prediger, Anke Wischgoll
paper_link: "https://doi.org/10.1007/s10857-026-09765-y"
abstract: >-
  Many teachers seek to foster students' conceptual
  understanding, e.g., for meanings of multiplication, but are
  only partially prepared to go beyond surface translations
  between multiple representations. This concerns out-of-field
  teachers as well as in-field teachers (i.e., with mathematics
  teaching certificate). The paper presents a professional
  development program in which teachers engage in PD inquiry
  activities (a) to specify what understanding multiplication
  entails and how deeper connections of representations are
  articulated, (b) to monitor students' understanding and
  uncover surface translations, and (c) to enhance students'
  understanding by engaging them in explicating unit structures
  in graphical and symbolic representations. The randomized
  controlled trial with n = 94 teachers investigates the extent
  to which out-of-field and in-field teachers' approximated
  practices can change in a PD session, and whether their
  professional learning gains depend on an explicit summary in
  the PD systematization phase. The comparison of pre-vignettes
  and post-vignettes reveals significant professional learning
  gains for all three practices, slightly more strongly for
  out-of-field teachers. For out-of-field teachers, the explicit
  summary in the systematization phase has significant
  additional effects for specifying and enhancing, less for
  monitoring practices. The findings show that out-of-field
  teachers can undergo very promising changes, especially when
  including an explicit systematization phase on specifying the
  mathematical learning content.

      Detailed Analysis:

      Following the prescribed decision tree, the first question
      is whether the intervention adds extra time or budget to
      one arm relative to the other. It does not: both arms share
      the same 70-min inquiry phase, the same 20-min transfer
      phase, and an equal 30-min systematization phase. The only
      difference is the mode of systematization (open
      breakout-room discussion versus a 10-min expert video plus
      note-taking), which is the treatment variable being tested,
      not an added block of time or resources. Time on task and
      overall educational input are therefore matched across the
      two randomised groups, and no meaningful resource imbalance
      exists.

      Criterion B is met because both conditions received
      equivalent time and resources, differing only in the
      systematization mode that constitutes the treatment
      contrast.
