title: >
  The Effects of Flipped Classroom versus Lecture-Based
  Learning on Academic Performance and Perceptions of
  Classroom Environment among Nursing Students: A
  Randomized Controlled Trial
authors: >
  Mahboubeh Sadat Yousefi, Mohsen Fooladzadeh Dehghan,
  Mahnaz Ilkhani, Rahimeh Khajoei, Saeide Heidari,
  Mozhgan Jokar
paper_link: "https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-09746-y"
abstract: >-
  Introduction: Nursing students often struggle with the
  fluids and electrolytes course because of the
  complexity, extensive detail, and fleeting nature of
  the content. This study aimed to compare the effects of
  two teaching approaches, the flipped classroom and
  traditional lecture-based learning, on nursing
  students' academic performance and perceptions of the
  classroom environment. Methods: This was a
  single-blind, parallel-group, pretest-posttest
  randomized controlled trial. The study was conducted
  with 58 nursing students at Qom University of Medical
  Sciences, Iran, during the 2019-2020 academic year.
  Participants were randomly assigned using a
  computer-generated block randomization sequence to
  either the intervention group (flipped classroom,
  n=29) or control group (lecture-based learning, n=29).
  The primary outcome was academic performance, measured
  by researcher-developed 20-item multiple-choice
  knowledge test. The secondary outcome was perceptions
  of the classroom environment, assessed using the
  College and University Classroom Environment Inventory
  (CUCEI). Results: After controlling for pretest scores,
  ANCOVA revealed a significant and large effect of the
  flipped classroom on post-test knowledge (F = 104.719,
  P < 0.001, partial eta squared = 0.65). Conclusion:
  The findings suggest that the flipped classroom
  approach can improve nursing students' academic
  performance and perceptions of the classroom
  environment. Trial registration: The study was
  retrospectively registered in the Iranian Clinical
  Trials Registry (IRCT) with the trial number
  IRCT20240610062078N2 on December 21, 2025.

      Detailed Analysis:

      Both arms received the same amount of in-class
      time (four two-hour sessions), the same
      instructor, the same learning objectives and core
      content, and identical PowerPoint slides (the
      control group receiving them after each session
      for review). The one resource difference is that
      the flipped-classroom group additionally engaged
      with pre-class videos and questions requiring
      about 60-80 minutes per session, delivered via
      Telegram. Applying the criterion B decision tree:
      extra time/engagement is present and is not
      negligible, so the key question is whether it is
      integral to the intervention being tested. The
      flipped classroom is defined by shifting
      foundational content delivery to pre-class
      self-study so that in-class time is freed for
      active application; the pre-class preparation is
      therefore an inseparable, defining component of
      the intervention, not a detachable add-on that
      could have been balanced without dissolving the
      "flipped" design. The control condition represents
      the business-as-usual lecture baseline against
      which this integral design is contrasted, and it
      received the same slides. Under ERCT logic, when
      the additional resource is integral to the
      intervention tested against business as usual, the
      criterion is met, though the extra pre-class time
      and the intervention-only Telegram channel are
      acknowledged by the authors as potential
      confounders.

      Criterion B is met because the only resource
      difference (pre-class engagement time) is integral
      to the flipped-classroom intervention being tested
      against a business-as-usual lecture control that
      received identical in-class time, instructor,
      content, and slides.
