authors: >

abstract: >-
  Teaching English as a Foreign Language (EFL) is constantly
  searching for the most successful learning method. Using active
  learning methodologies and ICT tools in educational contexts
  entails some changes in the teaching-learning process. This
  research work analyzes the effect of academic performance with a
  digitalized active learning methodology applied during two months
  in the compulsory secondary education stage. The sample of the
  study is composed of sixty students aged between 15 and 16 years
  old (M: 15.18; DE: 0.39) from an educational institution in
  Madrid (Spain) who have been randomly assigned into a control and
  an experimental group. The control group is instructed following
  a traditional teaching with teacher-centered lectures whereas the
  experimental group follows a digitalized active learning
  methodology (gamification). The results in the final test show a
  significantly higher academic performance in the experimental
  group. This finding indicates that the use of a digitalized
  active learning methodology such as gamification prepares
  secondary education students better for the final exam. This
  exploratory study proposes an L2 teaching methodology with a
  positive effect on the learning process and academic performance
  which can be applicable to other school grades.

      Detailed Analysis:

      The paper is internally contradictory about randomisation.
      The abstract claims students "son asignados aleatoriamente"
      (were randomly assigned) to control and experimental groups,
      but the design section explicitly states the study is
      quasi-experimental ("diseño cuasi-experimental") because
      group assignment "no es al azar" (is not random): the two
      groups are pre-existing intact classes formed when students
      entered secondary school years before the study. The only
      random element for this study is which of the two intact
      classrooms received the experimental condition ("la
      asignación del grupo control y experimental a las aulas fue
      aleatoria"). With only two intact clusters in a single
      school, no described randomisation procedure for the classes
      themselves, and the authors' own explicit self-designation of
      the design as quasi-experimental, this cannot be considered a
      properly implemented class-level RCT under the ERCT standard.
      The intervention is whole-class gamified teaching, not
      personal tutoring, so the tutoring exception does not apply.
      Because the study itself is not a properly documented RCT
      (see rct field), this also affects the top-level randomised
      controlled trial classification.

      Criterion C is not met because the authors explicitly
      describe a quasi-experimental design with two pre-existing
      intact classes rather than a properly implemented and clearly
      described class-level randomised controlled trial.

      Applying the criterion B decision tree: the gamified
      intervention was delivered inside the regular English class
      sessions (twenty 50-minute sessions in the same trimester),
      with the same teacher, the same curricular content, and the
      same morning time slot for both groups (alternating between

      methodology itself and is not a resource withheld from the
      control group's business-as-usual instruction. Because
      instructional time, teacher, content and setting were
      matched, and the methodological differences are integral to
      the intervention contrast being tested, the groups are
      considered balanced.
