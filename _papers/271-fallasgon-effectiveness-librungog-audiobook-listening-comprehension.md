abstract: >-
  This study investigates the impact of the Lib-RUNGOG Audiobook
  intervention on the listening comprehension skills of Grade 7
  students. Employing a randomized pre-test post-test control group
  design, the study compares the performance of an experimental
  group exposed to the audiobook intervention with a control group
  utilizing traditional listening strategies. The research
  instruments include a Mother Tongue-Based Multilingual Education
  (MTB-MLE)-integrated audiobook, pre-test, and post-test items.
  Data analysis involves t-tests for independent and correlated
  samples, with a significance level of 0.05. The findings reveal
  a significant improvement in the experimental group's post-test
  scores, emphasizing the efficacy of the Lib-RUNGOG Audiobook
  intervention. In contrast, the control group employing
  conventional strategies shows no significant improvement.

      Detailed Analysis:

      Criterion C requires that randomisation be conducted at the
      class level (or stronger), not among individual students
      within a single class. In this study the treatment was
      assigned to intact class sections: Section 1 received the
      Lib-RUNGOG Audiobook intervention and Section 2 received the
      conventional listening strategy, and the paper explicitly
      states that "the manner of selecting which section will serve
      as the experimental or control group was also done through
      simple random sampling." The random drawing of 20 students
      from each section was a sampling step (choosing participants),
      not the treatment assignment; the unit of treatment
      assignment was the whole section (class). Although only two
      classes were involved, which is statistically very weak, the
      criterion as written asks whether entire classes rather than
      students within one class were randomised to conditions, and
      that is the case here. Contamination between treatment and
      control students within a single classroom is avoided because
      each condition was applied to a separate intact section.

      Criterion C is met because the two intact class sections were
      randomly assigned to the experimental and control conditions,
      i.e., randomisation occurred at the class level.

      Criterion B requires the control group to receive balanced
      time and resources so that the specific intervention effect
      is isolated. Applying the current decision tree: the first
      question is whether the intervention group was given extra
      time or budget relative to the control group at all. Here
      both sections followed the same overall procedure (pre-test,
      a single listening activity, notes/reflections, post-test)
      during the same class time; the only difference is the
      content medium used during that shared listening slot
      (Lib-RUNGOG audiobook vs. the conventional listening
      strategy). The paper states both groups were "exposed to the
      same conditions except for the intervention program," and
      Table 2's data-collection sequence is identical for both
      arms. Since no additional instructional time, budget, or
      materials beyond swapping the listening medium were given to
      the experimental group, this falls into the "no extra
      resources present" branch of the decision tree, which is met
      by default without needing to assess whether the difference
      is the treatment variable. Even if the audiobook medium were
      treated as an "extra resource," it is clearly integral to
      the treatment being tested (the whole point of the study is
      comparing the audiobook medium to the conventional strategy
      within the same time slot), so the criterion would still be
      met under the treatment-variable branch.

      Criterion B is met because both groups received the same
      class time and procedural structure, with the audiobook
      medium being the treatment contrast itself rather than an
      unmatched additional resource.
