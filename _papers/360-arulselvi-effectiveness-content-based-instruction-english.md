abstract: >-
  This study investigated the effectiveness of the
  Content-based instruction (CBI) method over the
  conventional method in learning Optional 1 English among
  College of Education students in the Salem District of
  the Tamil Nadu state in southern India. A randomized
  pretest, post-test, control group and experimental group
  design were employed. Students from the university formed
  both the control and experimental groups. Each group
  consisted of 30 learners. The t-test analysis revealed
  that for teaching English to these students who are
  non-native English speakers, CBI was more effective than
  the conventional method and the CBI students developed
  their language skills as well as gaining access to new
  concepts through meaningful content.

      The paper's own methodology section explicitly
      describes a "non-randomized" design, with the
      experimental group formed by ranking students on
      achievement scores rather than by random assignment,

    quote: >-
      "In the present study, the non-randomized control

    analysis: >-
      Relevant Quotes:

      1) "In the present study, the non-randomized control
      group pretest, post-test design was adopted." (p. 45)

      2) "The subjects of the two groups were selected and
      the application of randomness led to the
      classification of the control and experimental
      groups." (p. 45)

      3) "The experimental group was formed on the basis of
      the academic achievement scores of the students.
      Thirty learners were grouped into five teams with six
      members in each team based on the scores of the first
      term examination of the English language. The first
      five highest scorers formed the first members of the
      five teams and the remaining scorers were distributed
      to each team as per the procedure of distribution."
      (p. 45)

      4) "The control group consisted of 30 learners
      studying in the same class of the same college."
      (p. 45)

      Detailed Analysis:

      The abstract claims "A randomized pretest, post-test,
      control group and experimental group design were
      employed," but this directly contradicts the
      Methodology section, which explicitly states the
      design was "non-randomized." The description of how
      the experimental group was formed (ranking students by
      achievement score and distributing them across five
      teams to balance ability) is a deliberate, stratified
      assignment procedure, not random allocation. The
      control group was simply "the same class of the same
      college," with no described procedure for randomly
      allocating students between control and experimental
      conditions. No quote anywhere in the paper describes
      randomisation at the class, school, or even student
      level being genuinely carried out; the concrete
      account given is of a non-randomized, quasi-
      experimental design using pre-formed, ability-matched
      groups. The intervention is not a personal tutoring
      program, so the student-level exception does not
      apply either.

      Because the study explicitly self-describes as
      "non-randomized" and no valid random-assignment
      procedure at any level is documented, this raises
      serious doubt about whether the paper qualifies as a
      genuine RCT at all, and criterion C is not met.
