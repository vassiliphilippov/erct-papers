abstract: >-
  The orthographic forms (spellings) of second language (L2) words
  and sounds affect the pronunciation and awareness of L2 sounds,
  even after lengthy naturalistic exposure. This study investigated
  whether instruction could reduce the effects of English
  orthographic forms on Italian native speakers' pronunciation and
  awareness of L2 English sounds. Italians perceive, produce, and
  judge the same sound as a short sound if it is spelled with one
  letter and as a long sound if it is spelled with a digraph, due
  to L1 Italian grapheme-phoneme correspondence (GPC) rules whereby
  double consonant letters represent long consonants. Totally, 100
  Italian learners of English were allocated to two conditions
  (final n = 88). The participants in the explicit GPC (EGPC)
  condition discovered English GPC rules relating to sound length
  through reflection, explicit teaching, and practice; the
  participants in the passive exposure condition practiced the same
  words as the EGPC participants, but with no mention of GPCs. Pre-
  and postintervention production (delayed word repetition) and
  phonological awareness (rhyme judgment) tasks revealed no
  positive effects of the instruction. GPC instruction appears to
  be ineffective in reducing orthographic effects on L2 phonology.
  Orthographic effects may be impervious to change, whether by
  naturalistic exposure or by instruction.

      Detailed Analysis:

      Criterion P requires pre-registration of the full study
      protocol (hypotheses, methods, planned analyses) on a
      registry before data collection began. The paper reports
      ethics approval and shares task and intervention materials on
      OSF and IRIS, but sharing materials is not protocol
      pre-registration: no trial registry (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov,
      ISRCTN, OSF Registrations) is cited, no registration ID or
      date is given, and no statement of pre-registration appears
      anywhere in the paper.

      Internet-backed verification: the OSF nodes referenced in the
      paper were checked directly via the OSF API. The node at
      https://osf.io/eubpz is titled "Bassetti, B., Cerni, T., &
      Masterson, J. (2021) Materials for 'Efficacy of a teaching
      intervention...'", is categorized as an ordinary "Project"
      (not a Registration), was created 2021-01-18, and its OSF
      metadata explicitly returns "registration": false, confirming
      it is a materials-sharing repository, not a pre-registered
      protocol. No trial registry entry for this study (e.g. on
      ClinicalTrials.gov, ISRCTN, or OSF Registries) was located.
      Notably, several planned elements (a second teaching session,
      a delayed posttest) were changed or dropped, with no
      pre-registered protocol against which such deviations could
      be checked.

      Criterion P is not met because no pre-registration of the
      study protocol is mentioned in the paper, and a direct check
      of the linked OSF repositories confirms they are unregistered
      materials projects rather than a pre-registration.
