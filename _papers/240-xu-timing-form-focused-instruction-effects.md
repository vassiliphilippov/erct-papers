title: >
  Timing of form-focused instruction: Effects on EFL learners'
  grammar learning
authors: >
  Jinfen Xu, Changying Li
paper_link: "https://doi.org/10.14746/ssllt.2022.12.3.4"
abstract: >-
  This study investigates how different form-focused instruction
  (FFI) timing impacts English as a foreign language (EFL)
  learners' grammar development. A total of 169 Chinese middle
  school learners were assigned to four conditions randomly:
  control, before-isolated FFI, integrated FFI, and after-isolated
  FFI. The three experimental groups received treatments which
  combined form and meaning with the English passive voice as the
  teaching target, but learners' attention was drawn to the
  passive voice with different timing. The before-isolated and
  after-isolated groups received the treatment before and after
  communicative activities, respectively. For the integrated FFI
  group, intervention occurred during communicative activities. A
  picture writing test and a written error correction test were
  employed to measure students' performance. The results indicated
  that the three experimental groups manifested significant
  improvement. Before-isolated FFI produced the best immediate and
  delayed effects, and integrated FFI produced better immediate
  effect than after-isolated FFI, while after-isolated FFI
  produced better delayed effect than integrated FFI. The findings
  indicated that pedagogical sequences in FFI are important, and
  teachers might need to guide adolescent learners to focus on
  form explicitly before communicative activities.

      The unit of treatment allocation was the intact class, not
      individual students within one classroom. Four whole classes
      were randomly chosen from the twelve eighth-grade classes in

      design is weak in that only four classes were involved (one
      class per condition) and the exact mechanics of assigning
      conditions to classes are only briefly described, but the
      paper does describe class-level random selection and states
      random assignment to conditions, satisfying the class-level
      requirement.

      allocation operated on whole classes rather than on

      Applying the criterion B decision tree: did the intervention

      instructional treatments were delivered during the students'

      requiring extra budget, or out-of-class resources; the

      timetable. All students in all four classes continued their

      FFI), were explicitly equalised in duration and intensity
      through scripted, video-checked lessons validated by five
      analysers. Under branch 1 of the decision tree (no extra
      time/budget present), the balance requirement is trivially
      satisfied because no group received additional time, staff
      attention, or materials budget relative to another.

      time or resources beyond regularly scheduled lessons, and
      the experimental conditions were strictly matched in
