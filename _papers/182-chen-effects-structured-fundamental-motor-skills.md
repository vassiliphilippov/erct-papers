title: >
  Effects of a structured fundamental motor skills
  intervention on executive function in second grade
  students: a randomized controlled trial
authors: >
  Cheng Chen, Xiang Gao, Xuyan Cui, Wenjuan Zhang
paper_link: "https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-47218-x"
abstract: >-
  Given the relative scarcity of randomized controlled
  trial evidence regarding systematic fundamental motor
  skills interventions for lower primary school students,
  this study aimed to examine the effectiveness of a
  10-week structured fundamental motor skills intervention
  on the executive function and its subcomponents in
  second-grade students. Between September and November
  2023, a randomized controlled trial was conducted.
  Eighty-nine second grade students were assigned to
  either an experimental group (n=44) or a control group
  (n=45). The experimental group received a 10-week
  fundamental motor skills intervention, consisting of
  three sessions per week, while the control group
  maintained their regular physical activities. The
  Chinese version of the Executive Function Scale was used
  for assessment before and after the intervention. After
  the intervention, students in the experimental group
  demonstrated significantly better performance than those
  in the control group in Sustained Attention, Working
  Memory, Planning and Initiation, Self-Regulation,
  Inhibitory Control, Emotion Regulation, and the total
  executive function score. This study confirms that a
  structured fundamental motor skills intervention can
  effectively promote the development of multiple core
  executive functions in second-grade students.

      Detailed Analysis:

      Although the paper uses cluster sampling language to
      select schools and classes, the unit of treatment
      randomisation was the individual student. The two
      selected classes were the pool from which students
      were drawn, and the 100 eligible students within
      these classes were then individually allocated 1:1 to
      experimental or control conditions using a random
      number table. This means students in the same
      classroom were split between intervention and control,
      which is student-level randomisation within a class,
      not class-level or school-level assignment. The ERCT
      C criterion requires randomisation of entire classes
      or schools to prevent contamination, unless the
      intervention is one-to-one tutoring (which this is
      not; it is a group physical education program). Since
      treatment and control students co-existed within the
      same two classes, the design risks contamination and
      does not satisfy class-level randomisation.

      Criterion C is not met because the actual treatment
      allocation was at the individual student level within
      the same classes, not at the class or school level,
      and the intervention is not a personal tutoring
      exception.
