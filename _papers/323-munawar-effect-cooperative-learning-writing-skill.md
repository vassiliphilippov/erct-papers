abstract: >-
  Cooperative learning may have beneficial impact on
  classroom teaching. The study aimed to identify the
  effect of cooperative learning in writing ability of the
  7th class students in the subject of English. The
  population of the study consisted of 68 students enrolled
  at elementary level (7th grade) in Government Girls High
  School Burj Attari, Sheikhupura. There were two sections
  of 7th grade in the school and 34 students in each
  section. The study was Quasi Experimental with a
  Non-equivalent control group, pre-test and post-test
  design. One section was randomly selected as experimental
  group (X1) and the other was assigned to control group
  (X2) on the basis their previous class results. The
  results describe there is a significant difference
  between post-test scores of control and experimental
  group. Findings of the current study reveal that
  cooperative learning method is more effective than the
  grammar translation method among students. It is
  recommended that teachers might apply cooperative
  learning techniques to improve students' writing skill in
  the classroom.

      1) "The study was Quasi Experimental with a

      design." (Abstract, p. 35)

      2) "One section was randomly selected as experimental
      group (X1) and the other was assigned to control
      group (X2) on the basis their previous class
      results." (Abstract, p. 35)

      3) "The Quasi-experimental (non-equivalent groups)
      pre-test, post-test, control group design was used."
      (p. 38)

      4) "The intact groups were randomly assigned to the
      treatments." (p. 38)

      5) "One section was randomly assigned to experimental
      group (X1) and the other was assigned to the control
      group(X2)." (p. 38)

      Detailed Analysis:

      The unit of assignment was the intact class section,
      not individual students, which structurally matches
      the class-level requirement. However this is
      undermined by two facts: (a) the authors explicitly
      and repeatedly self-classify the design as "Quasi
      Experimental" with a "Non-equivalent control group"
      (stated identically in both the Abstract and the
      Methodology), which is inconsistent with a genuine
      RCT; (b) the Abstract states groups were selected on
      "the basis their previous class results," i.e. by
      matching prior achievement rather than by a
      randomisation procedure, directly contradicting the
      Methodology's separate claim that "intact groups were
      randomly assigned." No randomisation procedure (e.g.
      coin flip, random number generator, or lottery) is
      described anywhere in the paper.

      Given the paper's own repeated self-identification as
      a non-equivalent-groups quasi-experiment and the
      contradictory, undocumented assignment procedure,
      this cannot be considered a properly implemented
      random assignment at the class level.

      Final: Criterion C is not met because the paper
      self-identifies as a non-equivalent-groups
      quasi-experiment with assignment based on prior
      achievement, not documented randomisation.
